High-pressure Raman spectroscopic study of a copper-oxide system

Na₂Cu₂TeO₆ was studied under pressure to examine the evolution of its vibrational modes and identify spectral changes that could motivate further structural investigation.

01
Used a diamond anvil cell with a metallic gasket, ruby pressure marker and a 4:1 methanol–ethanol pressure-transmitting medium.
02
Worked with a 532 nm laser, edge filter and grating-based spectrometer for Raman collection and detection.
03
Performed pressure-dependent Raman measurements extending to approximately 12 GPa and analysed the spectra using Origin.
04
Observed redistribution of intensity between low-frequency peaks and merging/new features near 7 GPa; these were treated as indications requiring further HP-XRD confirmation, not as a definitive phase-transition claim.

Experimental workflow

From sample
to spectrum

A compact account of the preparation, calibration and measurement sequence described in the project report.

01
Cell preparation

Gasket indentation and sample chamber

A stainless-steel gasket was pre-indented and a central hole was prepared as the DAC sample chamber before cleaning and mounting.

02
Pressure environment

Sample, ruby and hydrostatic medium

The sample was loaded with ruby as an in-situ pressure calibrant and a 4:1 methanol–ethanol mixture to provide quasi-hydrostatic conditions.

03
Optical alignment

Raman measurement under pressure

The DAC and Raman optical path were aligned carefully before collecting spectra at successive pressure points.

04
Analysis

Pressure evolution of phonon modes

Peak positions and intensities were compared across pressure to identify systematic spectral evolution and features for further investigation.
