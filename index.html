Indium micro-bumps for qubit–readout integration

IIT Bombay · Quantum hardware case study

Fabricating microscopic metal connections to join quantum-device chips while preserving the integrity of their electrical interfaces.

Role
Junior Research Fellow
Focus
3D quantum-device integration
Methods
Lithography · sputtering · electroplating
Lab
IIT Bombay

Ongoing work on flip-chip interconnects and cleanroom fabrication at IIT Bombay.

The challenge

Chips in a quantum device need to exchange signals. I develop small indium interconnects to join the chips at their interfaces.

My approach

  1. Prepare the interfaces

    Prepare wafers and seed layers using cleanroom fabrication and sputtering.

  2. Pattern the device

    Define bump locations with optical lithography and use wet or dry etching where needed.

  3. Fabricate indium bumps

    Use indium electroplating to develop micro-bumps for the interconnects.

  4. Integrate the chips

    Develop the architecture for flip-chip bonding and 3D quantum-device integration.

The integration goal

Bring separate qubit and readout chips together in a compact 3D architecture.

IZO/Ag/IZO transparent electrodes for tandem solar cells

Tandem solar cells

Project detailsProject details

Modelled transmittance over 300–800 nm · 78.7% IZO and 85.2% IZO/Ag/IZO

IZO / Ag / IZOSetfosThin-film opticsNCPRE

Transparent electrodes have to balance conductivity and light transmission. I studied multilayer stacks of indium zinc oxide and ultrathin silver for tandem solar cells.

The work combined RF sputtering, thermal evaporation, Setfos optical modelling, UV–Vis–NIR spectroscopy, sheet-resistance measurements, and interference and admittance modelling.
