NCPRE · Indian Institute of Technology Bombay

IZO/Ag/IZO as an oxide–metal–oxide transparent electrode

The thesis used SETFOS and Python to explore how layer thickness, ultrathin-silver optical behaviour and electrical constraints interact. The project began in photovoltaics but became increasingly focused on thin-film interference, nanoscale optical constants and light–matter interaction.

01
Modelled transmission, reflection and absorption across single-junction and tandem-relevant spectral ranges.
02
Calculated transmitted photon flux and theoretical short-circuit-current limits under the AM1.5G spectrum.
03
Studied thickness-dependent, non-bulk optical behaviour in sub-10 nm silver using Drude-based corrections and LSPR-related interpretation.
04
Compared optical gains with sheet resistance and Haacke figure-of-merit requirements.

Modelling workflow

From optical
constants to design

The project combined optical modelling, independent analysis and fabrication-aware interpretation.

01
Material inputs

Wavelength-dependent optical constants

Prepared and compared optical-constant datasets for IZO and silver, including corrected treatment of ultrathin Ag rather than relying only on bulk values.

02
SETFOS

Layer-thickness sweeps

Systematically varied top IZO, silver and bottom IZO thicknesses to identify promising optical stacks.

03
Python analysis

Photon-weighted performance

Calculated average transmission, photon flux, theoretical current caps and comparative performance metrics.

04
Physical interpretation

Growth, continuity and plasmonic behaviour

Connected the simulated optical response to Volmer–Weber island growth, film continuity, wetting-layer effects and localized surface plasmon resonance considerations.

Experimental realism

Why fabricated
films differ

The most important conclusion was not only which stack performed best, but how to interpret the gap between simulation and experiment.

Continuity

Sub-10 nm silver can form islands before becoming a continuous conducting film.

Morphology

Roughness, grains and thickness non-uniformity introduce scattering and broaden optical response.

Interfaces

Interfacial diffusion and imperfect boundaries change both electrical and optical behaviour.

Measured constants

Real deposited films may differ from the optical constants assumed in simulation.
